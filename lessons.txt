Pithy Lessons:

2.1 *Specifications: a spec is a map from expanding inputs to refining outputs*.
What could I learn? (Growing inputs)
What can I say? (Table)
What keeps my word? (Refining outputs)
"Something you have yet to learn could make you break a promise". Commitments are Promise Keepers.
> The input order says what you may still learn. The table says which answers are permitted. The output order says what an emitted answer promises. You need a commitment exactly when something you may still learn would make a permitted answer break a promise you've already made. The election shows each part: ballots still to arrive are the input order, the winner rule is the table, and whether ⊥ claims "no winner yet" or "no winner ever" is the output order.

2.3: *Lamport is Watching You.* If somebody can find out what you said, it can be communicated via Lamport's Telephone -- and it can change the future (of inputs).

2.3: *Consistency was always confluence, no matter what you wanted it to be."

2.4: Functional CALM: Consistent Means Monotone.

2.4 (Corollary "state is fixed by input"): For functional specs, the only thing that matters is which input you reach. 
There are two spec questions: is a current output exposable (Lamport's Surveillance), and is there a maximal input (Eventual Consistency).

§4 Distributed Execution

    4.1: distribution adds nothing to the criterion.
    4.1: convergence comes free from any consistent functional specification; a CRDT merge is not required.
    4.2: Locks, timestamps, Paxos: different accents, same two words (seal and choose).
    4.3: 
    - commitment, coordination and distributed coordination are three different things.
    Commitment changes what's possible. A commitment needs coordination when another run could contradict it, and distributed coordination when that run is at another participant. Waiting is irrelevant; "impatience" -- i.e. sealing input --- is a commitment.
    - Waiting is free. Promising is cheap. Promising for someone else is expensive.
    - 2PC's unanimity vote detects agreement rather than creating it: the coordinator is a referee, not a matchmaker. Only its timeout abort is a commitment, and that one stays at the coordinator.
    4.4: a partition doesn't change which commitments you need; it removes the ability to make them jointly. (A partition doesn't change your promises; it takes away your phone.)
    4.4: decide what must be emittable (explicit) separately from what may merely be observed (implicit).
    4.4: a linearizable read is a commitment against a write the reader hasn't seen (Gilbert–Lynch).

§5 Composition

    Coordination is non-monotone, yet coordinated systems are consistent.
    Coordinate once, upstream. The log's commitments are all that state-machine replication needs.
    A stage may need a commitment even when the whole pipeline doesn't.
    Moving the deadline into the input doesn't remove the promise; it moves who makes it (timer/punctuation upstream seals; the tally downstream only retires).
