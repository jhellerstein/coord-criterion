\section{Coordination-Free Frontiers}
\label{sec:frontiers}

We ask how strong a specification can be before coordination is needed,
holding the inputs, input order, and output order fixed.  A specification $T$
is \emph{stronger} than $S$ when $T\subsetneq S$.  This compares sets of rows
and is unrelated to $\OutputOrd$, which compares outputs.

\subsection{Remembered outputs}

For $A,B\subseteq O$, the \emph{Hoare preorder} is
        \begin{equation}
  A\HoareOrd B
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall a\in A\;\exists b\in B:\;a\OutputOrd b.
  \label{eq:lifted-output-order}
        \end{equation}
Fix a table $X\subseteq I\times O$, with fibers $X(i)$ possibly empty.  A
\emph{powerset relaxation} of $X$ is a map $A:I\to\mathcal P(O)$ with
$X(i)\HoareOrd A(i)$ for every $i$; it satisfies \emph{Relational
Monotonicity} (Definition~\ref{def:relational-monotonicity}) when
$i\InputOrd k$ implies $A(i)\HoareOrd A(k)$.  The \emph{remembered output} is
        \begin{equation}
  \widehat X(i)=\bigcup_{j\InputOrd i}X(j).
  \label{eq:monotone-hull}
        \end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Remembered-output frontier]
\label{thm:monotone-hull}
$\widehat X$ is a powerset relaxation of $X$ satisfying Relational
Monotonicity, and every such relaxation $A$ satisfies
        \begin{equation}
  \widehat X(i)\HoareOrd A(i)
  \qquad\text{for every }i.
  \label{eq:hull-coverage}
        \end{equation}
If every $\widehat X(i)$ is finite and $M(i)$ is its set of maximal elements,
then $M(i)\HoareOrd\widehat X(i)\HoareOrd M(i)$, $M$ satisfies Relational
Monotonicity, and $M(i)$ is the least subset $Y\subseteq\widehat X(i)$ with
$\widehat X(i)\HoareOrd Y$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$X(i)\subseteq\widehat X(i)$, and $i\InputOrd k$ gives $\widehat
X(i)\subseteq\widehat X(k)$.  For Equation~\ref{eq:hull-coverage}, let
$o\in X(j)$ with $j\InputOrd i$.  Relaxation gives $a\in A(j)$ with
$o\OutputOrd a$, and Relational Monotonicity gives $b\in A(i)$ with
$a\OutputOrd b$.

In a finite poset every element lies below a maximal one, so $\widehat
X(i)\HoareOrd M(i)$; the converse holds since $M(i)\subseteq\widehat X(i)$.
Then $i\InputOrd k$ gives $M(i)\HoareOrd\widehat X(i)\HoareOrd\widehat
X(k)\HoareOrd M(k)$.  If $Y\subseteq\widehat X(i)$ and $\widehat X(i)\HoareOrd
Y$, each $m\in M(i)$ lies below some $y\in Y\subseteq\widehat X(i)$, and
maximality gives $y=m$; so $M(i)\subseteq Y$.  Minimality does not use
finiteness.
\end{proof}

When $X=S$ is a specification, every $\widehat X(i)$ is nonempty.  Let
$A\equiv_H B$ mean $A\HoareOrd B\HoareOrd A$, and order the quotient
$(\mathcal P(O)\setminus\{\varnothing\})/{\equiv_H}$ by the order that
$\HoareOrd$ induces on classes.  Then $i\mapsto\widehat X(i)$, read in this
quotient, is a functional specification.  It is monotone, hence consistent by
Theorem~\ref{thm:functional-calm}.  The theorem says it is least, under
$\HoareOrd$ up to $\equiv_H$, among relaxations satisfying Relational
Monotonicity, and $M$ is its least representative drawn from remembered
outputs.

Table~\ref{tab:lookup-lift} gives an example: a data item moves from node $a$ to
node $b$ as input grows from $i_0$ to $i_1$.  Output $o_a$ ($o_b$) says that a
lookup from $a$ ($b$) succeeds, and $o_{a\leadsto b}$ says that $b$ holds the
item and $a$ forwards to it, so $o_a,o_b\OutputOrd o_{a\leadsto b}$.  The table
$X$ records only what is exact at each input: after the move, a lookup from $b$
succeeds and $a$ is no longer a location.  It violates Relational Monotonicity,
since $o_a$ at $i_0$ has nothing above it in $X(i_1)$.  The remembered output
repairs this by keeping the stale claim $o_a$ beside $o_b$; both are maximal,
so $M=\widehat X$.  Forwarding, $A(i_0)=\{o_a\}$ and
$A(i_1)=\{o_{a\leadsto b}\}$, is another relaxation satisfying Relational
Monotonicity, and Equation~\ref{eq:hull-coverage} places $\widehat X$ below it:
$\{o_a,o_b\}\HoareOrd\{o_{a\leadsto b}\}$.

\subsection{Persistent forwarding}

B-link trees keep a search starting at a split node correct by following a
right link, so the parent can be repaired later~\cite{lehman1981efficient}.  We
abstract the invariant.  Inputs are finite legal restructuring histories $h$,
ordered by prefix.  The structure after $h$ is a finite directed graph whose
nodes are \emph{positions}; each edge is a \emph{child edge} or a \emph{link
edge}, and some positions hold keys.  For each key $k$, every position has at
most one outgoing \emph{search edge for $k$}, the edge a search for $k$
follows there; the \emph{search for $k$ from $\pi$} follows search edges for
$k$ from position $\pi$.  An \emph{occurrence} of $k$ is a position holding
$k$, and \emph{current} means present after $h$.  The \emph{claim} $(k,\pi)$
belongs to $C(h)$ when the search for $k$ from $\pi$ reaches a current
occurrence of $k$ after finitely many edges.  Each restructuring step must
satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item positions keep stable identities;
  \item if $\pi\to\pi'$ was the search edge for $k$ at $\pi$ before the step,
        the search for $k$ from $\pi$ reaches $\pi'$ after it; and
  \item from every position that held $k$ before the step, the search for $k$
        reaches a current occurrence of $k$ after it.
\end{enumerate}
Deletion and reclamation can violate (2) and (3) and are excluded.

\begin{proposition}[Persistent-forwarding monotonicity]
\label{prop:persistent-forwarding}
Under (1)--(3), $h\InputOrd h'$ implies $C(h)\subseteq C(h')$.  The functional
specification $h\mapsto C(h)$, ordered by inclusion, is therefore monotone and,
by Theorem~\ref{thm:functional-calm}, consistent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By induction it suffices to treat one step.  Let the search for $k$ from $\pi$
reach an occurrence before the step.  After the step, by (2) the search from
each position on that path reaches the next one, so the search from $\pi$
reaches the old occurrence, and by (3) it continues to a current occurrence.
\end{proof}

Let $X(h)\subseteq C(h)$ be the claims that are \emph{exact} at $h$: their
search follows only child edges.  Order claims by equality, so that $\HoareOrd$
is inclusion.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:persistent-forwarding}, $C$ is a
powerset relaxation of $X$ satisfying Relational Monotonicity, so
Equation~\ref{eq:hull-coverage} gives
        \begin{equation}
  \widehat X(h)\subseteq C(h):
  \label{eq:forwarding-hull}
        \end{equation}
every claim that was ever exact remains valid, possibly through link edges.
Here $X$ has empty fibers, which is why Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone-hull} is
stated for tables.

\subsection{Preserving independent local choices}

Fix a finite participant set $P$ with $|P|\ge 2$.  Coordinates are indexed by
participants.  For each $p$ let $(W_p,\le_p)$ be a poset and, for each input
$i$, let $V_p(i)\subseteq W_p$ be nonempty: the \emph{views} $p$ may expose at
$i$.  A \emph{partial tuple} assigns to each $p$ either a value in $W_p$ or
$\bot$; partial tuples are ordered coordinate-wise, with $\bot$ below every
value.  $x$ is a \emph{restriction} of a complete tuple $y$ when each $x_p$ is
$\bot$ or $y_p$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{product interface} $S$ has a row $(i,x)$ for every
        restriction $x$ of every $y\in\prod_pV_p(i)$.
  \item A \emph{normalized strengthening} $T$ selects, at each input, a set of
        tuples in $\prod_pV_p(i)$ and has as rows exactly their restrictions.
        It is \emph{locally conservative} when for every $i$, $p$, and $v\in
        V_p(i)$ some selected tuple $z$ at $i$ has $z_p=v$.
  \item The \emph{owner deployment} (Definition~\ref{def:deployment}) of a
        specification over partial tuples lets $p$ emit exactly its rows whose
        tuple is $\bot$ outside coordinate $p$.  Participant $p$ is the sole
        owner of coordinate $p$ and owns no other.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Independent-product frontier]
\label{thm:product-frontier}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If views continue (for $i\InputOrd j$ and $v\in V_p(i)$ there is $w\in
        V_p(j)$ with $v\le_p w$), the product interface is monotone across
        partitions (Definition~\ref{def:monotone-across-partitions}) under the
        owner deployment, so every separable run family is confluent at every
        common \indesc of its participants' current inputs
        (Proposition~\ref{thm:complete-cap-deployment}).
  \item If each $V_p(i)$ is an antichain, no strict, normalized, locally
        conservative strengthening $T\subsetneq S$ is monotone across
        partitions under its owner deployment.  (Here
        Definition~\ref{def:monotone-across-partitions} is applied to $T$:
        read $T(j)$ for $\fiber{j}$.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Take an authorized participant run family and a common \indesc $j$ of its
current inputs.  We construct $w\in\prod_pV_p(j)$ that \outrel every emitted
output; the construction does not use separability.  If $p$ has a run ending at
current input $k_p$, its remainder table has a row $(k_p,u)$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:run-residual} $u$ \outrel every output $p$ emitted.  As $u$ is a
restriction of some $y\in\prod_qV_q(k_p)$, continuation gives $w_p\in V_p(j)$
with $y_p\le_p w_p$.  Otherwise pick any $w_p\in V_p(j)$.  Each output $p$
emitted is $\bot$ outside coordinate $p$, and its value there is at most
$u_p\le_p y_p\le_p w_p$.

(2) Strictness gives a row $(i,x)\in S\setminus T$, with $x$ a restriction of
some $y\in\prod_pV_p(i)$ that $T$ does not select at $i$.  By local
conservativity, the partial tuple $e_p$ that is $y_p$ at $p$ and $\bot$
elsewhere is a row of $T$ at $i$, authorized for $p$.  Let every participant
run from the canonical state at $i$ and emit $e_p$.  Under any partition, two
participants in different components emit, so the family is separable; take
$j=i$.  A common \outnoun $u\in T(i)$ is a restriction of a selected $z$, and
$y_p\le_p u_p=z_p$ with both in $V_p(i)$, so $y=z$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The hypotheses matter.  The deployment is essential: if some $V_p(i)$ has two
views with no common upper bound in $V_p(i)$ (for instance, two distinct views
when $V_p(i)$ is an antichain), the product interface has no Lifted Monotonicity, since
those two rows have no common \outnoun; with $A_p=S$ for every $p$, the two
rows form a separable family.  Owning only its own coordinate is needed: let
two replicas each expose configuration 0 or 1 at every input, with views
ordered by equality (so each $V_p(i)=\{0,1\}$ is an antichain), and let $T$
require them to agree, keeping $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$.  This $T$ is strict and
locally conservative, so by part (2) it is not monotone across partitions
under its owner deployment.  If instead one participant owns both coordinates,
the other may emit only the empty tuple, which every output refines, and $T$ is
monotone across partitions; forbidding even that makes the deployment
single-authority (Appendix~\ref{app:deployment}).  Neither part says whether a participant can emit
its views during a partition; that is Deployment Availability
(Definition~\ref{def:deployment-availability}), a property of the
implementation.

\paragraph{Causal register.}
Take the causal register of Appendix~\ref{app:registers}.  Let $V_p(h)$ be the
maps from the reads of replica $p$ in $h$ to writes of $h$ or $v_0$ that some
serialization for $p$ explains, ordered by extension; each $V_p(h)$ is an
antichain, since its maps share a domain.  Its product interface, whose output
at $h$ is a tuple of one view per replica, is the specification in which each
replica answers all of its reads at once, and the owner
deployment lets each replica answer only its own reads.  Views continue by the
argument given there, so part (1) applies.

Linearizability selects the tuples that a single total order of all
operations explains.  It is locally conservative: a serialization $L_p$ for
$p$, together with $\to$, has no cycle, because $L_p$ already extends $\to$ on
its operations and $\to$ is transitive; so some total order of all operations extends both, and it
gives $p$'s reads the same sources as $L_p$.  The two-write history of
Appendix~\ref{app:registers} shows that it is strict, so part (2) recovers, for the
product form of linearizability, the obstruction
of Appendix~\ref{app:registers}.  This depends on the input
recording only causal precedence.  If the input also records real-time
precedence, a write that finished before $p$'s read in real time but lies
outside its causal past must precede that read in every linearization, so some
causal view of $p$ alone is excluded; linearizability is then not locally
conservative and part (2) does not apply.
