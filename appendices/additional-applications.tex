\section{Additional Applications}
\label{app:additional-applications}

Each example below identifies an obstruction in a specification.  None
replaces model-specific results about failures, computability, or
termination.

\subsection{Committed authority, then monotone evidence}

Suppose a commitment fixes an initial authority and a rule determining each
certified successor.  The system then exposes a certified epoch chain $E$,
a set of votes or certificates $V$, and a set of decisions $D$ indexed by
epoch and slot (in Paxos or Raft: terms, quorum certificates, log positions),
ordered by
\begin{equation}
  (E,V,D)\OutputOrd(E',V',D')
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  E\preceq_{\rm pref}E',\quad V\subseteq V',\quad D\subseteq D'.
  \label{eq:authority-refinement}
\end{equation}
Protocol safety guarantees that later evidence \outrel earlier evidence, so
authority changes and certified decisions are \outnouns and need no further
commitment.  This covers following the succession rule, not choosing values:
competing proposals for an undecided slot have incompatible extensions of
$D$, so certifying one is a commitment.  If the succession rule were not
fixed, competing next epochs would require one as well.

\subsection{$k$-set agreement needs finite families}

Fix $k\geq1$, participants $p_1,\ldots,p_{k+1}$ (possibly among others), and
distinct proposals $v_1,\ldots,v_{k+1}$.  Outputs are partial decision maps
$m:P\rightharpoonup V$ ordered by extension, with at most $k$ values in their
range.  At $p_r$'s proposal input, $\{p_r\mapsto v_r\}$ is permitted, and the
union of the proposal inputs is a common \indesc.  Any $k$ of these $k+1$
rows have a common \outnoun there; all $k+1$ do not, since it would have
$k+1$ values.  So the common-\indesc condition fails and the specification
is not monotone (Proposition~\ref{prop:common-expansion-form}); a partition
separating the $p_r$ gives the negative side of Complete CAP.  Consensus is
$k=1$.  Classical impossibility results additionally depend on the
computation, failure, and progress model~\cite{saks2000set}.

\subsection{Tight renaming}

Inputs are finite participant sets $P$, ordered by inclusion.  Outputs are
finite partial naming maps ordered by extension, and the outputs at $P$ are
the injective maps
\begin{equation}
  m:P\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,|P|\}.
  \label{eq:tight-renaming}
\end{equation}
At $\{p\}$ the only output is $p\mapsto1$, and at $\{q\}$ it is $q\mapsto1$.
Each has a \outnoun at $\{p,q\}$, so Relational Monotonicity does not fail
here, but no injective map extends both.  Directed Outputs also fails at
$\{p,q\}$: $\{p\mapsto1,q\mapsto2\}$ and $\{p\mapsto2,q\mapsto1\}$ have no
common \outnoun.  A partition separating $p$ and $q$ gives the obstruction of
Proposition~\ref{thm:complete-cap-deployment}.  Extending a completed naming
is not a witness: a new process can take name $n+1$.  The obstruction comes
from independent partial memberships.  The renaming hierarchy adds wait-free
and topological conclusions~\cite{herlihy1991waitfree,herlihy1999topological}.

\subsection{Snapshots}

Inputs are finite downward-closed causal histories ordered by induced
extension.  The \emph{prefix} specification outputs any finite downward-closed
subset of the current history.  Old prefixes stay legal as history grows, and
finite unions of prefixes are prefixes, so it satisfies Lifted Monotonicity;
it promises neither freshness nor completeness.  A specification that
promises the complete state at a finished boundary, with outputs ordered by
equality, changes its required output when a further relevant event arrives,
so by Functional CALM the boundary must be sealed
before the output is exposed.  Both are safety statements; a snapshot
protocol must also collect a consistent cut and terminate under its channel
and failure assumptions~\cite{chandy1985distributed}, and wait-free atomic
snapshots~\cite{afek1993atomic} show that the process, memory, and failure
model matter, not total ordering alone.
