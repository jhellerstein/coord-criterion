\section{Proofs and Details for Composition}
\label{app:composition}

This appendix proves the results of Section~\ref{sec:composition}, defines
composition with independent inputs and its curried evaluation, and works out
the replicated store with bare reads.

\subsection{Sequential composition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}]
We verify the common-\indesc condition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:common-expansion-form}).  Let rows $(i_r,o_r)$ of
$S_2\circ S_1$, finitely many, have common \indesc $j$, and choose $m_r$ with
$(i_r,m_r)\in S_1$ and $(m_r,o_r)\in S_2$.  The condition for $\Spec_1$ gives
$m\in S_1(j)$ \outrelpart every $m_r$.  Then $m$ is a common \indesc of the
inputs of the rows $(m_r,o_r)$, and the condition for $\Spec_2$ gives
$u\in S_2(m)$ \outrelpart every $o_r$.  So $(j,u)\in S_2\circ S_1$ is the
required common \outnoun.
\end{proof}

Tables of functions are closed under $\circ$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}
specializes to functions.

\begin{corollary}[Functional composition]
\label{cor:functional-composition}
If $f:(I,\InputOrd)\to(M,\preceq_M)$ and
$g:(M,\preceq_M)\to(O,\OutputOrd)$ are monotone, so is $g\circ f$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The tables of $f$ and $g$ satisfy Lifted Monotonicity and compose to the
table of $g\circ f$; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Composition with independent inputs}
\label{sec:parameterized-composition}

Sequential composition cannot express a downstream component with input of
its own, such as a replica that serves bare reads
(Section~\ref{sec:composition}), so we give each component a separate
input.  Let
\begin{align*}
  \Spec_F&=\bigl(I\times A,\preceq_I\times\preceq_A,B,\preceq_B,F\bigr),\\
  \Spec_G&=\bigl(J\times B,\preceq_J\times\preceq_B,C,\preceq_C,G\bigr)
\end{align*}
be ordered relational specifications sharing the ordered domain
$(B,\preceq_B)$, which carries values from $F$ to $G$.  Here $i\in I$ is input
that only $F$ receives, and $j\in J$ is input that only $G$ receives.  We
split $F$'s input as $(i,a)$ so that $F$ restricted to a fixed $i$ is a table
from $A$ to $B$.  The curried evaluation below uses this split to evaluate
the composition one pair $(i,j)$ at a time.  The \emph{independently
parameterized composition} of $\Spec_F$ and $\Spec_G$ is
\begin{align}
  G\star F
  &=\pi_{I,J,A,C}\bigl(F\Join_{B}G\bigr) \notag\\
  &=\{((i,j,a),c):\exists b.\ ((i,a),b)\in F
                         \land ((j,b),c)\in G\},
  \label{eq:parameterized-composition}
\end{align}
with the product order on $I\times J\times A$.  Since $F$ is total on
$I\times A$ and $G$ is total on $J\times B$, every composed input has a
nonempty fiber.

\begin{lemma}[Independently parameterized composition]
\label{lem:parameterized-composition}
If $\Spec_F$ and $\Spec_G$ satisfy Lifted Monotonicity, then the specification
with table $G\star F$ satisfies Lifted Monotonicity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let rows $((i_r,j_r,a_r),c_r)$, finitely many, have common \indesc $(i,j,a)$,
with witnesses $b_r$ from Equation~\ref{eq:parameterized-composition}.  The
common-\indesc condition for $\Spec_F$ at $(i,a)$ gives $b\in F(i,a)$
\outrelpart every $b_r$.  Then $(j,b)$ is a common \indesc of the
$(j_r,b_r)$, and the condition for $\Spec_G$ gives $c\in G(j,b)$ \outrelpart
every $c_r$.  So $((i,j,a),c)\in G\star F$ is the required common \outnoun.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Curried evaluation.}
For fixed parameters let
\begin{equation}
  F_i=\pi_{A,B}(F[I=i]),
  \qquad
  G_j=\pi_{B,C}(G[J=j]),
  \label{eq:curried-fibers}
\end{equation}
with the selection of Section~\ref{sec:functional-specifications}.

\begin{proposition}[Curried equijoin plan]
\label{prop:curried-query-plan}
The table in Equation~\ref{eq:parameterized-composition} equals
\begin{equation}
  \bigcup_{(i,j)\in I\times J}
  \left\{((i,j,a),c):
    (a,c)\in\pi_{A,C}\bigl(F_i\Join_B G_j\bigr)\right\}.
  \label{eq:curried-query-plan}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Equation~\ref{eq:curried-fibers}, $((i,a),b)\in F$ and $((j,b),c)\in G$
iff $(a,b)\in F_i$ and $(b,c)\in G_j$.  Quantifying over $b$ gives the same
condition on $((i,j,a),c)$ in both expressions.
\end{proof}

For monotone $f:I\times A\to B$ and $g:J\times B\to C$, the instance of
Lemma~\ref{lem:parameterized-composition} for their tables says that
\begin{equation}
  h(i,j,a)=g\bigl(j,f(i,a)\bigr)
  \label{eq:curried-composition}
\end{equation}
is monotone; in curried form, $h^\sharp(i,j)=g^\sharp(j)\circ f^\sharp(i)$,
which is the plan of Proposition~\ref{prop:curried-query-plan} on tables of functions.

\subsection{Jointly monotone composites}

If every stage satisfies Lifted Monotonicity, a guarded composite whose first
stage runs under the empty basis emits only what $\mathcal S_2\circ\mathcal
S_1$ emits under the empty basis.  Its runs are runs of a Lifted-Monotone
specification, at whose states every output is exposable
(Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-boundary}).  So the next stage again has an
empty-basis guard, and by induction no stage of the pipeline commits.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-monotone}]
(1) is Lemma~\ref{lem:composition}, and also the case $D=M$ of (2).

(2) Let $D$ contain the outputs of $\Spec_1$.  Take rows $(i_r,o_r)$ of $S_2\circ
S_1$ with a common \indesc $j$, and intermediate values $m_r$ with
$(i_r,m_r)\in S_1$ and $(m_r,o_r)\in S_2$.  The common-\indesc condition for
$\Spec_1$ gives $m\in S_1(j)$ that \outrel every $m_r$.  All of $m_r$ and $m$
lie in $D$, so the condition for $\Spec_2$ on $D$ gives $o\in S_2(m)$ that
\outrel every $o_r$, and $(j,o)\in S_2\circ S_1$.  No closure property of $D$
is used.

(3) The table $S_2\circ S_1$ of Equation~\ref{eq:spec-composition} mentions
no order on $M$, and Lifted Monotonicity of the composite uses only the orders
on $I$ and $O$.  So the composite under $\preceq_M$ is the composite under the
other order, which satisfies Lifted Monotonicity by (1).  The same holds after
re-encoding the intermediate values.  A re-encoding is a map $e:M\to M'$
together with a decoding $e':M'\to M$ that recovers every value,
$e'(e(m))=m$.  Replace $S_1$ by $\{(i,e(m)):(i,m)\in S_1\}$ and $S_2$ by
$\{(m',o):(e'(m'),o)\in S_2\}$.  A join through $e(m)$ is a join through
$e'(e(m))=m$, so the composite table is unchanged, whatever the orders on $M$
and $M'$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The reach of case (3).}  Let $f:I\to M$ and $g:M\to O$ be
functions with $g(f(\cdot))$ monotone.  Place $a$ below $b$ in $M$ when
$g(a)\OutputOrd g(b)$.  This relation is reflexive and transitive.  It makes
$g$ monotone by construction, and $f$ monotone because $g(f(\cdot))$ is.  It
can place two distinct values each below the other, so it is an order only
when no such pair exists, in particular when $g$ maps distinct values to
distinct values.  Then case (3) applies.  In general it need not.  Let $I$ be
the chain $x\InputOrd y\InputOrd z$, $M=\{p,q\}$, $f(x)=f(z)=p$, $f(y)=q$, and
$g$ constant.  The composite is constant, hence monotone.  Under any order on
$M$, monotonicity of $f$ gives $p\preceq_M q\preceq_M p$, so $p=q$, a
contradiction.  By the exposure test for tables of functions, $f(i)$ is
exposable at $\sigma(i)$ exactly when $f(i)\preceq_M f(k)$ for every
$k\InputGe i$, so the upstream under the empty basis cannot emit at one of the
canonical states of $x$ and $y$.  The stages can be repaired only by merging
$p$ and $q$ into one intermediate value.

For the capped counter of Section~\ref{sec:composite-calm}, let $\mathcal S_1$
be the table of the function $c(n)=\min(n,9)$ on $(\mathbb N,\le)$ and
$\mathcal S_2$ the table of the test $t(n)=[n<10]$, whose two outputs are
incomparable.  $\mathcal S_2$
violates Lifted Monotonicity, since $[9<10]$ and $[10<10]$ differ, but it
satisfies it on $\{n\le 9\}$, so the composite is Lifted-Monotone by case (2).  At
$\sigma_{\mathcal S_2}(m)$ with $m\le 9$, emitting $[m<10]$ must seal the row
$(10,\mathit{false})$, so $\mathcal S_2$ cannot emit under the empty basis.
Let its basis contain the seal of $\{n\ge 10\}$, applied at its first output.
In the guarded composite every input that a run of $\mathcal S_2$ accepts or
emits at occurs in an output sequence of $\mathcal S_1$, and none is
$\ge 10$.  A store commitment that removes rows only at such inputs is never
contradicted, so the store coordinates at most once.  At least once holds by
the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-coordination}(2), since every row of a
state of $\mathcal S_1$, and every \indesc that exposability supplies, lies in
$\{n\le 9\}$.  The guarded composite is proper, and after its first output the
store answers every count without commitment.

For the double complement, let $M$ be the subsets of a nonempty set $U$.  The
table of a function $f$ has $f(i)$ exposable at $\sigma(i)$ exactly when
$f(i)\preceq_M f(k)$ for all $k\InputGe i$.  For the complement under
inclusion this holds only for $i=U$, whose complement is $\emptyset$.  So under
the empty basis the first stage emits only $\emptyset$, at the canonical state
of $U$.  Ordered by reverse inclusion, the complement is monotone in both
stages, and by case (3) the composite is the identity in either order.

Let $\mathcal P$ have one input $*$ and table $\{(*,x),(*,y)\}$ with $x,y$
incomparable, and let $f$ map both to one value.  The composite
$\mathcal K\circ\mathcal P$ has one row, so it satisfies Lifted Monotonicity.
At the canonical state of $\mathcal P$ neither $x$ nor $y$ is exposable, and
under the empty basis an output event is legal only for an exposable output
(Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-commitment-normal-form}).  Input events only remove
rows at other inputs, and there are none, so every run of $\mathcal P$ under
the empty basis stays at the canonical state and emits nothing.  The store
then receives nothing.  Renaming $x,y$ to $\mathit{lo}\preceq\mathit{hi}$ makes
$\mathcal P$ Lifted-Monotone, since $\mathit{hi}$ retires $\mathit{lo}$, and by case
(3) the composite is unchanged.

\subsection{Proper Coordination}

Throughout, $\mathcal K$ satisfies Lifted Monotonicity, used through the
common-\indesc condition, and $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal B_{\mathcal K}$ are
the bases of the systems for $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal K$.  For tables $R$ of
$\mathcal P$ and $T$ of $\mathcal K$ we write $R\Join T=\{(k,x):\exists m.\
(k,m)\in R\land(m,x)\in T\}$.  The image of the canonical state
$\sigma_{\mathcal P}(i)$ is the canonical state of $\mathcal K\circ\mathcal P$
at $i$, since $P[\mathrm{in}\InputGe i]\Join K$ is the composite table
restricted to the \indescs of $i$.

Two facts about a state $\tau$ that $\mathcal K$ reaches without commitment
carry the proofs.  First, every input $j\succeq_M\tau.\mathrm{cur}$ has a row
in $\tau$: the common-\indesc condition gives $u\in K(j)$ that \outrel an
output $v$ at the current input, and $(j,u)$ survives, since every output
emitted so far is \outrelpast by $v$.  Second, every output at the current
input is exposable (Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-boundary}).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:guarded-runs}]
(1) After $\Out{\gamma}{m}$ every row \outrel $m$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-commitment-normal-form}), so the outputs
$m_1\preceq_M m_2\preceq_M\cdots$ of a run form a chain.  The store starts at
$\sigma_{\mathcal K}(m_1)$, which is reached without commitment.  By the two
facts it can receive each $m_{r+1}\succeq_M m_r$ and answer it by an exposable
output, so its run is a run under the empty basis.

(2) Before $\mathcal P$'s first output we keep $\rho.\mathrm{cur}=\sigma.\mathrm{cur}$
and $R_\sigma\Join K\subseteq R_\rho$, where $\sigma$ is $\mathcal P$'s state
and $\rho$ the composite's.  Afterwards we keep $R_\sigma\Join T\subseteq
R_\rho$, with $T$ the store's table, and every row $(k,m)$ of $R_\sigma$ has
$m$ above the store's current input.  Both hold at the start, where the image
of $\sigma_{\mathcal P}(i)$ is $\sigma_{\mathcal K\circ\mathcal P}(i)$.  For
$\mathsf{input}(j)$ of $\mathcal P$, a row $(j,m)$ of $R_\sigma$ and, by the
first fact, a row $(m,x)$ of $T$ give $(j,x)\in R_\rho$, so the input is legal,
and both events keep the rows at \indescs of $j$.  For a legal
$\Out{\gamma}{m}$ at input $i$, containment gives $(i,m)\in R_\sigma$ and a
resulting table inside $R_\sigma[\mathrm{out}\succeq_M m]$.  The store
receives $m$ and answers $o$, so $(m,o)\in T$ and $(i,o)\in R_\rho$.  The
composite's $\mathsf{output}(o)$ keeps $R_\rho[\mathrm{out}\OutputGe o]$, and
the store keeps $T[\mathrm{in}\succeq_M m,\mathrm{out}\OutputGe o]$, so the
join stays inside the composite's table.

(3) Let the runs of $\mathcal P$ end at $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n$, the
stores at $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n$, and the composite runs at
$\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_n$, and let each $\sigma_r$ continue under some basis to a
common state with a row $(j,m)$.  Events never add rows, so $(j,m)\in
R_{\sigma_r}$, and $m$ is above each store's current input.  Let $v_r$ be an
output at the current input of $\tau_r$.  The common-\indesc condition gives
$u\in K(m)$ that \outrel every $v_r$, and $(m,u)$ survives in every $\tau_r$.
So $(j,u)\in R_{\rho_r}$ for every $r$, and the composite runs are confluent
at $j$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:confluence}).
\end{proof}

For a function store, the guarded composite's commitments are covered by
$\mathcal P$'s.  For a table $R$ of $\mathcal P$ write
$f(R)=\{(k,f(m)):(k,m)\in R\}$.  Let
$\Out{\gamma}{m}$ be legal at $\sigma$, let $\gamma$ leave $\sigma_c$, and
let $\rho$ be a composite state with $f(R_\sigma)\subseteq R_\rho$.  No row
$(k,f(m'))$ with $(k,m')\in R_{\sigma_c}$ is in $\Sealed_{f(m)}$ or
$\Rejected_{f(m)}$ at $\rho$.  If $m\preceq_M m'$ then $f(m)\OutputOrd f(m')$.
Otherwise legality gives $(k,u)\in R_{\sigma_c}$ with $m\preceq_M u$ and
$m'\preceq_M u$, and $(k,f(u))\in R_\rho$ retires $(k,f(m'))$.  So every row
that the composite's canonical event for $f(m)$ removes by commitment has had
every preimage in $R_\sigma$ removed by $\gamma$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-coordination}]
(1) is the second fact.  If $\Out{\delta}{o}$ is legal at $\tau$, then $o$ is
at the current input of $\tau$ and so of the larger table $\tau'$, and
containment (Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-commitment-normal-form}) puts the
original's resulting table inside $R_\tau[\mathrm{out}\OutputGe o]\subseteq
R_{\tau'}[\mathrm{out}\OutputGe o]$, where $\Out{\varepsilon}{o}$ ends.  For a
function store with current input $m$, every row $(k,f(k))$ has $m\preceq_M k$,
so $f(m)\OutputOrd f(k)$ and nothing is ruled out, at every state.

(2) Let $\Out{\varepsilon}{m}$ be legal at $\sigma$, so $m$ is exposable at
$\sigma$, and let $o\in K(m)$ and $(k,x)$ be a row of $\mathcal K(\sigma)$ not
\outrelpast by $o$, coming from $(k,m')\in R_\sigma$ and $(m',x)\in K$.  If
$m\preceq_M m'$, the common-\indesc condition for the rows $(m,o)$ and
$(m',x)$ at $m'$ gives $u\in K(m')$ with $o,x\OutputOrd u$.  Otherwise
exposability gives $(k,w)\in R_\sigma$ with $m,m'\preceq_M w$, and the
condition at $w$ gives $u\in K(w)$.  Either way $(k,u)$ is a row of
$\mathcal K(\sigma)$ that retires $(k,x)$, so $o$ is exposable.  When
$\mathcal B_{\mathcal K}$ is empty, every output event of the store is some
$\Out{\varepsilon}{o}$, which removes no row by commitment, so the store does
not coordinate redundantly.

(3) The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure-boundary} derives the
common-\indesc condition from exposability at canonical states alone.  If
Lifted Monotonicity fails for $\mathcal K\circ\mathcal P$, some $o$ with
$(i,m)\in P$ and $o\in K(m)$ is therefore not exposable at
$\sigma_{\mathcal K\circ\mathcal P}(i)=\mathcal K(\sigma_{\mathcal P}(i))$,
and by (2) no $m'$ with $o\in K(m')$ is exposable at $\sigma_{\mathcal P}(i)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:empty-store}]
(1) Let $\sigma$ be a state of the store and $\sigma'$ a state reached
without commitment, with the same current input and $R_\sigma\subseteq
R_{\sigma'}$.  If $\mathsf{input}(j)$ is legal at $\sigma$, a row of $R_\sigma$
at $j$ is in $R_{\sigma'}$, so the input is legal at $\sigma'$, and both events
keep the rows at \indescs of $j$.  If $\Out{\delta}{x}$ is legal at $\sigma$,
$\Out{\varepsilon}{x}$ is legal at $\sigma'$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-coordination}(1), with a larger resulting table.  Both
start at $\sigma_{\mathcal K}(m)$, so the containment holds after each event.
The replacement's strategy allows each erased event, since the original's
allowed it before erasure.

(2) Call a row $(k,x)$ \emph{used} when some run of $\mathcal K$ on outputs
$\mathcal P$ can emit emits $x$ at input $k$.  Consider a guarded composite
run of the replacement.  It is a run of $\mathcal K$ on such outputs, so every
row it emits at is used.  We build the original's run event by event, with the
same erasure, and keep two facts: the current inputs agree, and every used row
of the replacement's table is in the original's.  An input event
$\mathsf{input}(j)$ of the replacement is followed by an output $x$ at $j$, so
$(j,x)$ is used, is in the original's table, and makes the input legal there.
An output event $\Out{\varepsilon}{x}$ is allowed because the original's
strategy allows some $\Out{\delta}{x}$ after a history with the same erasure.
The strategy ignores commitments, so it allows $\Out{\delta}{x}$ after the
original's history.  The row $(\mathrm{cur},x)$ is used, hence in the
original's table, so the strategy, which never stalls, offers a legal
$\Out{\delta'}{x}$.  The original's events so far, with the run of
$\mathcal P$, form a guarded composite run in which this event applies
$\delta'$.  By at most once, $\delta'$ is not contradicted, so it removes no
used row, and both facts persist.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:pipelines}]
Let $\mathcal S_1$ have any system and $\mathcal S_2$ satisfy Lifted
Monotonicity.  By Corollary~\ref{cor:empty-store}(1), every guarded composite
run has one with an empty-basis store and the same trace.  Theorem~\ref{thm:guarded-runs}(2),
applied to it and a prefix of the run of $\mathcal S_1$, gives a run of
$\mathcal S_2\circ\mathcal S_1$, and the canonical event for each output
(Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical-commitment-normal-form}) makes it a run under the
canonical basis that emits the store's outputs.  So the outputs of guarded
composite runs are emitted by a system for $\mathcal S_2\circ\mathcal S_1$,
and every result of this section applies to a further stage $\mathcal S_3$
with that system as its guard.  By induction this holds for every stage of the
pipeline.  Composition is associative, so the composite is
$\mathcal S_n\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal S_1$ however it is bracketed.
\end{proof}

For the mutex store of Section~\ref{sec:proper-coordination}, let
$\mathcal P$ be the log specification of
Example~\ref{ex:committed-log-composition}, under its canonical basis with a
strategy that allows every event, and $\mathcal K$ the table of any monotone
function $f$ of the log.  For a set $Q$ of logs, removing every row whose input is in $Q$ is a
commitment of $\mathcal K$ at any state whose current input is not in $Q$ and
which has such a row.  It removes whole fibers, so it seals.  Let
$\mathcal B_{\mathcal K}$ contain this commitment for $Q$ the logs that start
with $b$.  At a state whose current log is nonempty, every remaining log has
the same first command, so this commitment is undefined there.

Let the store's strategy take the lock at its first event when the log is
empty, and apply nothing otherwise.  In one guarded composite run,
$\mathcal P$ emits the empty log $[\,]$ by $\Out{\varepsilon}{[\,]}$, and the
store, at $\sigma_{\mathcal K}([\,])$, emits $f([\,])$ by applying the
commitment.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-coordination}(1), $f([\,])$ was
exposable without it.  $\mathcal P$ can also emit $[b]$ at
$\sigma_{\mathcal P}([b])$ by its canonical event, so it feeds the store
$[b]$, an input the commitment sealed.  So the commitment is contradicted,
and the store coordinates redundantly.  Its replacement gains behaviour.  At
$\sigma_{\mathcal P}([b])$, $\mathcal P$ can emit $[\,]$ and then $[b]$.  The
replacement emits $f([\,])$, receives $[b]$, and emits $f([b])$.  The locking
store must take the lock at $[\,]$, which seals $[b]$, so it cannot receive
$[b]$.

Let the strategy instead take the lock only when its current log is nonempty.
The lock is undefined there, so every output event the store performs is
some $\Out{\varepsilon}{x}$.  The store applies no commitment, so it does not
coordinate redundantly.  Its strategy depends only on the inputs it has
received and always offers $\Out{\varepsilon}{x}$, so it meets the hypotheses
of Corollary~\ref{cor:empty-store}(2).  A strategy that insists on the lock
at a nonempty log also applies no commitment and ignores commitments, but it
stalls.  When $\mathcal P$ emits $[b]$ first, the store never emits, while its
replacement emits $f([b])$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:invisible-coordination}]
The hypothesis gives $f(R_{\sigma_c})=f(R_\sigma)$.  By the covering
argument with $\rho=f(\sigma)$, no row of $f(\sigma)$ is in $\Sealed_{f(m)}$ or
$\Rejected_{f(m)}$, so $f(m)$ is exposable at $f(\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

For the per-key store of Section~\ref{sec:proper-coordination}, let
$\mathcal P$ have one input with fiber $\{[a,b],[b,a]\}$, and let $f$ send a
log to the family of its subsequences on each key.  The two logs are
incomparable with no common \outnoun, so $[a,b]$ is not exposable.  When $a$
and $b$ are on different keys, $f([a,b])=f([b,a])$, the composite has one row,
and $f([a,b])$ is exposable.

The commitments of $\mathcal P$ do not themselves push forward.  Let
$\mathcal P$ have one input with fiber $\{x,y\}$, $x$ and $y$ incomparable, and
$f(x)=\mathit{lo}\OutputOrd\mathit{hi}=f(y)$.  The choice $c_y$ removes $x$
and keeps $y$.  Its image would remove $\mathit{lo}$ and keep $\mathit{hi}$,
violating Equation~\ref{eq:commitment-excludes}, so no commitment of the
composite has that effect.  In the composite, $\mathit{hi}$ is exposable at the
canonical state, since it retires $\mathit{lo}$.

Monotonicity of $f$ is needed.  Let $\mathcal P$ be the table of the identity
on $M$, which needs no commitment.  For $m\preceq_M m'$, its run
$\mathsf{output}(m)$, $\mathsf{input}(m')$, $\mathsf{output}(m')$ maps to a run
of the composite exactly when $f(m)\OutputOrd f(m')$.  A store that must
itself choose cannot delegate the choice either.  If $\mathcal P$ is the
identity on one input, it admits no commitment at all.  If the store relates
that input to two incomparable outputs, every output event of the composite at
its canonical state applies a commitment, and that commitment is the store's.

\begin{example}[Bare reads]
\label{ex:bare-reads}
We add bare reads to Example~\ref{ex:committed-log-composition}.  Take $I$
and $B$ the proposal streams and logs of that example, $A$ a one-point set,
and $J$ the histories of bare reads received by a replica, ordered by prefix.
Take $C$ the pairs of a response history for logged commands, ordered by
extension, and a set of bare-read responses, ordered by inclusion.  Let $F$ be
the log specification $\mathcal P$ of that example, with the one-point $A$
added to its input.  Let $G$ relate a bare-read history $j$ and a log $b$ to
the responses obtained by executing $b$, together with at least one response to
each bare read in $j$.  Responses are facts, so a set of them may answer one
read more than once.  As in Example~\ref{ex:committed-log-composition}, $F$
and $G\star F$ are non-monotone.

Suppose first that each bare-read response records the log position it read
from, as in ``$x=5$ as of entry 17.''  A longer log never contradicts such a
response, so $G$ is monotone.  By Complete CALM and the exposure boundary,
every output of $G$ at every state reachable without commitment is
exposable, so bare reads need no commitment of their own.  Suppose instead
that the specification demands the latest value, so that a read of $x$ must
return the value of $x$ after all of $b$.  Then a bare read can be
contradicted by an entry it has not seen, and $G$ is not monotone.  By the
exposure boundary some output of $G$ then needs a commitment of the store's
own.  Bare reads then need coordination, typically by going through the log.
\end{example}
